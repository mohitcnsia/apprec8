# NSO (National Science Olympiad) Exam Pattern Guide

**Audience:** Quest Content Authors & Reviewers  
**Source Material:** 11 past papers from SOF NSO (Class 3, 4, and 5; Level 1 & Level 2; 2023–2025)  
**Purpose:** Pattern guide for question writing, syllabus coverage, and Olympiad-level difficulty calibration.

---

## 1. Exam Structure & Marking Scheme

### Class 3 and Class 4 (Level 1)
- **Total Questions:** 35 | **Time:** 60 minutes | **Total Marks:** 40
- **Section 1: Logical Reasoning** — 5 questions (Q1–Q5), 1 mark each (Patterns, analogies, coding-decoding, mirror images, embedded figures, directions, Venn diagrams).
- **Section 2: Science** — 25 questions (Q6–Q30), 1 mark each.
- **Section 3: Achievers Section** — 5 questions (Q31–Q35), 2 marks each (Higher-order thinking skills, multi-step experiments, flowcharts, multi-variable classification).

### Class 5 (Level 1)
- **Total Questions:** 50 | **Time:** 60 minutes | **Total Marks:** 60
- **Section 1: Logical Reasoning** — 10 questions (Q1–Q10), 1 mark each.
- **Section 2: Science** — 35 questions (Q11–Q45), 1 mark each.
- **Section 3: Achievers Section** — 5 questions (Q46–Q50), **3 marks each** (Complex Venn diagrams, apparatus experiments, rock classification, astronomical charts, physiological systems).

### Level 2 (Class 3, 4, 5)
- **Structure:** 30–35 Questions (No Logical Reasoning section; questions are entirely Science + Achievers).
- **Focus:** Application-heavy, data interpretation from tables, graphs, and experimental set-ups. Almost no simple definition-based recall.

## 2. Topic Weightage & Distribution (Class 5)

Across the four recent Class 5 papers (2023–24 Sets A & B, 2024–25 Sets A & B), questions distribute consistently across four domains:

| Domain | Key Topics | Avg Qs per Paper |
|---|---|---|
| **Living World: Plants** | Seed germination conditions, parts of seed (cotyledon, embryo), seed dispersal mechanisms, vegetative reproduction (stem, root, leaf, spores), plant adaptations (aquatic, desert, insectivorous), agriculture practices (winnowing, harvesting). | 6–8 |
| **Living World: Animals** | Breathing mechanisms (lungs, gills, spiracles, moist skin), body coverings (scales, shells, feathers, fur/blubber), feeding & teeth, camouflage & mimicry, migration, life cycles (3-stage vs 4-stage), habitats & adaptations. | 6–8 |
| **Human Body & Health** | Skeletal & muscular system (joints: hinge, ball-and-socket, pivot; voluntary vs involuntary muscles), digestive system & teeth, nervous system (brain parts: cerebrum, cerebellum, medulla; eye & ear structure), nutrients & balanced diet, deficiency diseases, communicable diseases & prevention. | 8–10 |
| **Matter, Materials & Changes** | States of matter (molecular spacing, definite volume/shape, compressibility), changes of state (sublimation, condensation, evaporation), solutions & separation (sedimentation, decantation, filtration, chlorination), physical vs chemical changes, biodegradable vs non-biodegradable. | 5–7 |
| **Forces, Energy & Simple Machines** | Types of forces (friction, gravity, electrostatic, magnetic), mechanical energy (kinetic vs potential), energy transformations (electrical to sound, light to chemical), simple machines (Class 1, 2, 3 levers, pulleys, inclined plane, screw, wedge). | 4–6 |
| **Earth, Universe & Environment** | Earth layers (crust, mantle, core), rock types (igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic), atmosphere layers & composition, phases of the Moon, natural disasters (earthquakes, volcanoes, tsunamis), environmental pollution & global warming. | 6–8 |

## 3. Characteristic Question Styles (with Invented Examples)

NSO questions rarely ask for pure recall. They test application through 9 distinct formats:

### Style A: Scientific Analogy
Two related concepts are linked, requiring the student to apply the same biological or physical rule.
> *Invented Example:*  
> Refer to the given relationship and select the option that satisfies the same relationship:  
> **Grasshopper : Spiracles :: Earthworm : \_\_\_**  
> (A) Gills  
> (B) Moist skin *(Correct)*  
> (C) Lungs  
> (D) Tracheae

### Style B: Multi-Statement Evaluation
Presents 3–4 numbered statements; the student must deduce which combination is correct or incorrect.
> *Invented Example:*  
> Read the following statements regarding seed germination:  
> (i) Seeds require warm temperatures for germination.  
> (ii) Seeds cannot germinate underground without direct sunlight.  
> (iii) Cotyledons provide nourishment to the developing embryo.  
> (iv) Radicle develops into the baby shoot.  
> Which of the statements are INCORRECT?  
> (A) (i) and (iii) only  
> (B) (ii) and (iv) only *(Correct)*  
> (C) (i), (ii) and (iv) only  
> (D) (ii) and (iii) only

### Style C: Venn Diagram / Property Overlap
Entities are categorized by shared properties using overlapping circles or tables.
> *Invented Example:*  
> In a Venn diagram: Circle X = "Breathes through lungs", Circle Y = "Can fly in the air". Which animal belongs to the overlapping region of X and Y?  
> (A) Flying fish  
> (B) Bat *(Correct — mammal with lungs that flies)*  
> (C) Dragonfly  
> (D) Ostrich

### Style D: Flowchart / Dichotomous Key
Students follow a decision tree based on anatomical or physical traits.
> *Invented Example:*  
> Follow the flowchart:  
> `Is it a seed?` → Yes → `Dispersed by wind?` → Yes → `Does it have papery wings?` → **P**.  
> Identify P:  
> (A) Dandelion *(has silky hair, not wings)*  
> (B) Drumstick *(Correct — winged seed)*  
> (C) Coconut  
> (D) Xanthium

### Style E: Experimental Setup & Fair Testing
Interpreting an experiment with control variables (e.g., covered beakers, boiling water, light vs dark pots).
> *Invented Example:*  
> Ananya placed three identical pots of mustard seeds:  
> - Pot 1: Moist soil, warm room, dark box.  
> - Pot 2: Dry soil, warm room, sunny window.  
> - Pot 3: Moist soil, inside a freezer (0°C).  
> In which pot will the seeds successfully germinate?  
> (A) Pot 1 only *(Correct — has water, air, warmth; light not needed)*  
> (B) Pot 2 only  
> (C) Pot 1 and Pot 2  
> (D) Pot 3 only

### Style F: Unscramble the Word + Application
The question gives an anagram of a scientific term, then asks about its properties (not just the word).
> *Invented Example:*  
> Unscramble the given letters to name a vegetative plant part: **HMRZOIE** (RHIZOME).  
> Which plant reproduces using this part?  
> (A) Sweet potato  
> (B) Ginger *(Correct)*  
> (C) Bryophyllum  
> (D) Rose

### Style G: Two-Column Matching
Matching 4 items in Column I with 4 options in Column II.
> *Invented Example:*  
> Match Column I with Column II:  
> (a) Hinge joint — (i) Shoulder  
> (b) Ball-and-socket joint — (ii) Elbow  
> (c) Pivot joint — (iii) Wrist  
> (d) Gliding joint — (iv) Neck  
> *(a)-(ii), (b)-(i), (c)-(iv), (d)-(iii)*

### Style H: "Find the INCORRECT Statement" / "Odd One Out"
Testing for subtle misconceptions where three statements are sound and one is false.
> *Invented Example:*  
> Which of the following statements about respiration in animals is INCORRECT?  
> (A) Tadpoles breathe through gills.  
> (B) Adult frogs breathe through moist skin while underwater.  
> (C) Whales take in oxygen dissolved in sea water using gills. *(Correct answer: false statement — whales have lungs)*  
> (D) Insects take in air through spiracles along their body.

### Style I: Two-Blank Context
Fills two blanks with a pair of matching options.
> *Invented Example:*  
> In a germinating seed, the \_\_(i)\_\_ emerges first to form the root system, while the \_\_(ii)\_\_ grows upward into the shoot.  
> (A) (i) plumule, (ii) radicle  
> (B) (i) radicle, (ii) plumule *(Correct)*  
> (C) (i) cotyledon, (ii) seed coat  
> (D) (i) embryo, (ii) stoma

## 4. The Achievers Section (Hotspots & Traps)

In Class 5, Achievers questions are worth **3 marks each (15 marks out of 60 total)**. They separate rankers from average scores:
1. **Compressibility of Matter:** Comparing syringes with water (incompressible) vs air (compressible).
2. **Layering / Structural Diagrams:** Labelled diagrams of Earth's interior (crust, mantle, outer liquid core, inner solid core) or the eye/brain.
3. **Compound Environmental Questions:** Correlating pollution types with multiple causes simultaneously (e.g. Venn diagrams with water, air, and noise pollution).
4. **Food Web Disruption:** Removing one organism (e.g. secondary consumer) and predicting the domino effect on primary consumers and producers.
5. **Phase of the Moon Timelines:** Given a moon phase on day X (e.g., Waxing Crescent on 12th May), identifying the phase on day X+10 (e.g. Waning Gibbous).

## 5. Gap List: Science Quest Unit 1 vs Exam Patterns

### What Unit 1 Covers Today (`sq-u1: The Living World`):
- ✅ **Topic 0 (Living and Non-Living):** Signs of life, living vs man-made/natural non-living, once-living materials (wood, cotton, leather, coal).
- ✅ **Topic 1 (Plants):** Seed structure, conditions for germination (water, air, warmth), seed dispersal mechanisms (wind, water, animal hooks, explosion), vegetative reproduction (stem, root, leaf, spores).
- ✅ **Topic 2 (Animals):** Breathing organs (lungs, gills, spiracles, skin), body coverings (scales, shells, feathers, fur), migration (Siberian crane, Arctic tern, Monarch butterfly), teeth/feeding adaptations, camouflage.
- ✅ **Unit Boss:** Mixed reasoning questions across these 3 topics.

### Gaps to Cover in Future Units (Class 5 Roadmap):

1. **Unit 2: Human Body, Food & Health (HIGHEST PRIORITY for NSO)**
   - *Skeletal System:* Major bones, joint types (hinge, ball-and-socket, pivot, gliding, skull suture).
   - *Muscular System:* Voluntary, involuntary, and cardiac muscles.
   - *Nervous System:* Cerebrum, cerebellum, medulla oblongata functions; eye (cornea, pupil, iris, retina) and ear (ossicles, eardrum).
   - *Nutrition & Deficiency:* Carbohydrates, fats, proteins, vitamins (A, B, C, D), minerals (Iron, Iodine, Calcium); scurvy, rickets, beriberi, anaemia, goitre.
   - *Diseases:* Communicable (pathogens: bacteria, virus, fungi, protozoa; vectors: mosquitoes, flies) vs non-communicable.

2. **Unit 3: Ecology, Agriculture & Environment**
   - Food chains and food webs (producers, consumers, decomposers, trophic balance).
   - Aquatic plants (free-floating, fixed-floating, submerged).
   - Agricultural sequence (ploughing → sowing → irrigation → weeding → harvesting → threshing → winnowing → storage).
   - Pollution types, greenhouse effect, global warming, conservation.

3. **Unit 4: Matter & Materials**
   - Molecular arrangement in solids, liquids, gases; compressibility and density.
   - Changes of state (melting, freezing, evaporation, condensation, sublimation).
   - Physical vs chemical changes.
   - Solutions, solubility with heat, separation of mixtures.

4. **Unit 5: Forces, Energy & Simple Machines**
   - Forces: Gravity, friction (advantages/disadvantages), electrostatic, magnetic.
   - Energy forms & transformations (kinetic, potential, electrical, solar, mechanical).
   - Simple machines: Levers (Class 1, 2, 3), inclined plane, wedge, screw, wheel and axle, pulleys.

5. **Unit 6: Earth & the Universe**
   - Earth structure: Crust, mantle, outer core, inner core.
   - Rocks & Minerals: Igneous (granite, pumice, obsidian), Sedimentary (limestone, sandstone, shale), Metamorphic (marble, slate, quartzite).
   - Moon: Phases of the moon cycle (29.5 days), eclipses (solar and lunar).
   - Atmosphere: Troposphere, stratosphere (ozone), mesosphere.
