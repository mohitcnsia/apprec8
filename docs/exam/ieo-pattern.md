## 2. Exam Structure & Marking Scheme

### Class 3 and Class 4 (Level 1)
- **Total Questions:** 35 | **Time:** 60 minutes | **Total Marks:** 40
- **Section 1: Word and Structure Knowledge** — 20 to 22 questions (Q1–Q20/22), 1 mark each.
- **Section 2: Reading** — 8 to 10 questions (2 short passages or 1 passage + 1 factual notice/advertisement), 1 mark each.
- **Section 3: Spoken and Written Expression** — 5 questions (Q26–Q30), 1 mark each (Everyday conversational situations, polite requests, idioms).
- **Section 4: Achievers Section** — 5 questions (Q31–Q35), **2 marks each** (Total 10 marks = 25% of the paper). Higher-order vocabulary, multi-blank collocations, complex syntax.

### Class 5 (Level 1)
- **Total Questions:** 50 | **Time:** 60 minutes | **Total Marks:** 60
- **Section 1: Word and Structure Knowledge** — 30 questions (Q1–Q30), 1 mark each.
- **Section 2: Reading** — 10 questions (Q31–Q40: 2 passages × 5 questions), 1 mark each.
- **Section 3: Spoken and Written Expression** — 5 questions (Q41–Q45), 1 mark each.
- **Section 4: Achievers Section** — 5 questions (Q46–Q50), **3 marks each** (Total 15 marks = **25% of total score**). Advanced vocabulary, passive voice, multi-blank collocations, subtle shades of meaning.

### Level 2 (Class 3, 4, 5)
- **Total Questions:** 30–35 Questions.
- **Characteristics:** Zero simple recall. Heavy emphasis on dense reading passages, subtle contextual synonyms/antonyms, contextual idioms, and nuanced sentence corrections.

## 4. Characteristic Question Styles (with Invented Examples)

*Note: All examples below are original invented examples illustrating official IEO styles without copying any copyrighted questions.*

### Style 1: Sentence Error Identification ("Spot the Error")
A sentence is divided into three or four chunks (A, B, C, D) or includes a "No error" option. The student must pinpoint the grammatical violation.
> *Invented Example:*  
> Choose the part of the sentence that has an error:  
> `Neither of the players` / `were able to score` / `during the overtime.` / `No error`  
> (A) Neither of the players  
> (B) were able to score *(Correct — "Neither" takes singular verb "was")*  
> (C) during the overtime.  
> (D) No error

### Style 2: Part of Speech in Sentence Context
Tests grammatical function rather than memorized word definitions. A word that can be multiple parts of speech is underlined inside a full sentence.
> *Invented Example:*  
> Choose the correct part of speech for the underlined word:  
> "The train arrived **fast** on the platform, but the passengers walked slowly."  
> (A) Adjective  
> (B) Adverb *(Correct — modifies verb "arrived")*  
> (C) Conjunction  
> (D) Noun

### Style 3: Semantic-Clued Spelling
Unlike traditional spelling bees that test arbitrary words, IEO asks for the correct spelling of a word matching a specific semantic definition.
> *Invented Example:*  
> Choose the correct spelling of the word which means 'a person who travels to a sacred place':  
> (A) Pilgrem  
> (B) Pilgrim *(Correct)*  
> (C) Pillgrim  
> (D) Pealgrim

### Style 4: Fine Nuance & Semantic Distinction
All options belong to the same broad semantic category (e.g., movement or light), but only one fits the specific collocation or physical context.
> *Invented Example:*  
> Choose the correct option to fill in the blank:  
> "A sudden gust of wind blew through the open window, causing the candle flame to \_\_\_."  
> (A) tremble  
> (B) shiver  
> (C) flicker *(Correct — flames flicker; people shiver or tremble)*  
> (D) wobble

### Style 5: Question Tags
A recurring staple in Class 4 and 5 papers testing auxiliary inversion and polarity reversal.
> *Invented Example:*  
> Choose the correct option to fill in the blank:  
> "She rarely complains about the long commute, \_\_\_?"  
> (A) doesn't she  
> (B) does she *(Correct — "rarely" is negative, so tag must be positive)*  
> (C) did she  
> (D) isn't it

### Style 6: Direct to Indirect (Reported) Speech
Tests pronoun, tense, and infinitive shifts in reporting instructions, questions, and statements.
> *Invented Example:*  
> The coach shouted to the runner, "Do not slow down before the finish line!"  
> The coach warned the runner \_\_\_.  
> (A) that do not slow down before the finish line  
> (B) not to slow down before the finish line *(Correct)*  
> (C) to not slow down before the finish line  
> (D) that he should not have slowed down

### Style 7: Spoken Expression & Situational Idioms
Tests natural social English and fixed conversational idioms in multi-turn dialogues.
> *Invented Example:*  
> Choose the correct option to complete the conversation:  
> **Arjun:** "Do you know who will win the inter-school debate?"  
> **Kavita:** "Nobody can tell right now. Your guess is \_\_\_ mine."  
> (A) as well as  
> (B) better from  
> (C) as good as *(Correct — idiom: "your guess is as good as mine")*  
> (D) the best of

### Style 8: Dual-Statement Relationship (Reading Comprehension)
Beyond simple factual retrieval, tests analytical understanding of cause, effect, and textual dependencies between two assertions.
> *Invented Example:*  
> Read the two statements from the passage:  
> - Statement I: *The expedition was forced to abandon their campsite at dawn.*  
> - Statement II: *The river burst its banks following torrential midnight rain.*  
> (A) Statement I and Statement II are false.  
> (B) Statement I is the direct result of Statement II. *(Correct)*  
> (C) Statement II is the direct result of Statement I.  
> (D) Statement I contradicts Statement II.

### Style 9: Two-Blank Synthesis in Achievers Section
Combines grammar (preposition/article) with vocabulary or verb tense across two blanks.
> *Invented Example:*  
> Choose the correct option to fill in the blanks:  
> "The teacher \_\_\_ in her students a deep \_\_\_ for classical literature."  
> (A) installed, admiration  
> (B) instilled, appreciation *(Correct — "instilled ... appreciation")*  
> (C) inspired, appreciating  
> (D) initiated, appreciate

## 5. Achievers Section: Difficulty & Calibration

In Class 5 IEO, the Achievers Section contains **5 questions worth 3 marks each (15 marks = 25% of the total paper)**. In Class 4, it is 5 questions worth 2 marks each (10 marks).

### Linguistic Register
The Achievers section uses vocabulary and grammatical constructions that are significantly above standard school curricula. Analysis of recent papers reveals:
- **Sophisticated vocabulary:** *spurious, meticulous, strident, insouciant, pauperized, agglomeration, perfunctorily, rhapsodically, penurious, threadbare, corpulent, terse*.
- **Passive continuous syntax:** *"I think we are being followed"*, *"was being restored"*.
- **Nuanced adverbs of manner:** *raggedly, clumsily, impassively, sporadically*.
- **Collocational precision:** *instil a love for, cast aspersions on, weather the storm*.

### Design Rules for Quest Achievers Questions
1. **Never make them ungrammatical or obscure:** The sentence must be natural, polished British English.
2. **Double testing:** Every Achievers question should test either:
   - High-register vocabulary where distractors are plausible words of similar length/root.
   - A two-blank dependency where getting blank 1 right still requires precision on blank 2.
   - Passive or conditional voice in complex multi-clause sentences.

## 6. Comprehensive Gap Analysis vs English Quest Units 0–3

A direct comparison of the current English Quest codebase (`u0.js`, `u1.js`, `u2.js`, `u3.js`) against the 11 official IEO papers reveals why teachers and aspirants find English Quest "too easy".

### Gap Category A: Missing Topics (4 Concrete Examples)

1. **Sentence Error Identification ("Spot the Error")**
   - *IEO Reality:* Every paper has 3–4 questions dividing a sentence into chunks (A, B, C, D) requiring the student to find the error (e.g. incorrect pronoun case, subject-verb clash, wrong participle).
   - *English Quest Status:* **Completely absent.** All 430+ questions in Units 0–3 are standard fill-in-the-blank or multiple-choice selection. Students never practise scanning a whole sentence to spot an error.

2. **Dedicated Lexis (Synonyms, Antonyms, Clued Spelling)**
   - *IEO Reality:* 20% to 25% of Section 1 in every paper is direct vocabulary: Synonyms (*dearth, corpulent*), Antonyms (*terse, dauntless*), and definition-clued spellings (*urbane, affluence*).
   - *English Quest Status:* **0% vocabulary coverage.** Units 0–3 are 100% grammar mechanics (Sentence types, Parts of speech, Tenses, Subject-Verb Agreement). There is no vocabulary unit, no synonym/antonym nodes, and no spelling nodes.

3. **Spoken & Written Expression (Situational Dialogue & Idioms)**
   - *IEO Reality:* Every paper has 5 dedicated questions (Q41–Q45) testing social interaction, pragmatic conversational idioms (*cross that bridge when we come to it, your guess is as good as mine, in case*).
   - *English Quest Status:* **Zero dialogue questions.** English Quest sentences are isolated third-person statements (e.g. *"The dog barks at the gate"*). Students get zero practice with conversational pragmatics.

4. **Question Tags and Reported Speech**
   - *IEO Reality:* Recurring tested grammar topics in Class 4 and 5 (Q8, Q15 in 2023–24).
   - *English Quest Status:* Neither question tags (*"You don't agree with us, do you?"*) nor direct-to-indirect speech shifts (*"Mother asked me not to bring my bag"*) exist in Units 0–3.

### Gap Category B: Where Our Questions Are Easier Than the Papers (4 Concrete Examples)

#### 1. Verb Agreement: Single-Blank Recall vs Two-Blank / Inverted Syntax
- **English Quest (`eq-u3-compound-quiz` q1):**
  > *"Neither the teacher nor the students \_\_\_ in the hall."*  
  > Options: `were`, `was`, `is`, `are`  
  > *Analysis:* Straightforward single-blank proximity rule.
- **IEO Class 5 Level 1:**
  > *"Neither of the candidates \_\_\_ the criteria, yet both \_\_\_ invited for the interview."*  
  > Options: `meets / was`, `meets / were`, `meet / were`, `meet / was`  
  > *Gap:* IEO tests compound agreement across two interdependent clauses with contrasting polarity (*neither* = singular, *both* = plural).

#### 2. Vocabulary Distractors: Obvious Nonsense vs Fine Contextual Distinction
- **English Quest (`eq-u1-adverbs-quiz` q3):**
  > *"The snail moved \_\_\_ across the path."*  
  > Options: `slowly`, `loudly`, `angrily`, `hungrily`  
  > *Analysis:* Trivial common-sense filter; a 6-year-old knows snails are slow.
- **IEO Class 5 Level 1:**
  > *"The breeze caused the candle flame to \_\_\_."*  
  > Options: `waver`, `shiver`, `tremble`, `flicker`  
  > *Gap:* All 4 words mean slight unsteady movement. The question tests exact collocational precision (flames *flicker*, people *shiver*, voices *tremble*, decisions *waver*).

#### 3. Pronouns: Elementary Identification vs Indefinite / Reciprocal Subordination
- **English Quest (`eq-u1-pronouns-quiz` q2):**
  > *"Ravi is my friend. \_\_\_ loves reading books."*  
  > Options: `He`, `She`, `They`, `It`  
  > *Analysis:* Elementary gender/singular pronoun replacement.
- **IEO Class 5 Level 1:**
  > *"The playwright wrote the script \_\_\_, but allowed the actors to interpret the dialogue \_\_\_."*  
  > Options: `himself / themselves`, `himself / each other`, `itself / themselves`, `oneself / one another`  
  > *Gap:* Requires resolving reflexive pronouns across compound subjects in subordinate clauses.

#### 4. Reading Comprehension: Fact Retrieval vs Analytical Cause-and-Effect
- **English Quest Reading Nodes (e.g. Clock Tower):**
  > *"Who built the clock tower?"* or *"What colour was the dial?"*  
  > *Analysis:* Pure literal recall from the preceding paragraph.
- **IEO Class 5 Level 1:**
  > *"Statement I: ... Statement II: ... Choose the correct option: (A) Statement I is the direct result of Statement II; (B) Statement II contradicts Statement I..."*  
  > *Gap:* Requires evaluating rhetorical and logical relationships between ideas, inferring authorial motivation, and understanding idiomatic expressions used within the passage (*"Herculean task"*).

## 7. Actionable Recommendations for English Quest Roadmap

To prepare students realistically for IEO without compromising the engaging pedagogical tone of the app, we recommend:

1. **Unit 4: Vocabulary & Word Power (High Priority)**
   - Dedicate a full unit to Olympiad vocabulary:
     - Synonyms & Antonyms (Latin/Greek roots, high-register words).
     - Clued Spelling (spelling from definition).
     - Nuanced word pairs & collocations (flicker vs tremble, lash out vs punch).
2. **Unit 5: Advanced Syntax, Tags & Reported Speech**
   - Question tags with positive/negative reversals and semi-negative adverbs (*rarely, seldom, hardly*).
   - Direct to Indirect speech conversions.
   - Sentence Error Detection ("Spot the Error").
3. **Unit 6: Spoken and Written Expression**
   - Situational dialogues, polite register, common idioms and proverbs in everyday conversation.
4. **Upgrade Boss Battles & Gate Pools in Units 1–3:**
   - In Unit 1 (Parts of Speech): add "Underlined word in sentence context" questions.
   - In Unit 3 (Subject-Verb Agreement): add 2–3 two-blank questions per gate pool.
   - In all Unit Boss battles: reserve the final 3 questions as high-difficulty "Achievers" questions.
