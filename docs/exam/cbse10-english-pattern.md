# CBSE Class 10 English: grammar pattern (for Seema's Class 10 pilot)

Written by Claude on 2026-11-05 from two papers the owner shared. **Pattern only: no question is copied.** We write our
own questions in this style; the PDFs are not kept in the app or in git.
- CBSE "Competency Focused Practice Questions", Class 10 English: Grammar
- A compiled bank of past board questions: Section B (Grammar, then Letter writing)

## Where grammar sits in the board paper
- **Section B, Q3, Grammar: "any ten of twelve" tasks × 1 mark = 10 marks**, then a letter.
- Task forms: fill a blank with the right verb form (word in brackets); choose a determiner; **"Identify the error and
  supply the correction" (Error: ___ Correction: ___)**; pick the option that names the error and its fix
  (e.g. feel → feels); complete reported speech (statements, questions, requests, dialogues with a narrative frame);
  choose the passive / modal / tense / conditional form; complete a question tag.
- CBSE's competency questions go further: a sentence or dialogue in context, then "which change would alter the verb in
  part C", "how many errors are in this instruction", "which underlined phrase shows an action completed before a future
  time", "which statement shows the speaker is not sure".

## From the 2025 and 2026 board papers (owner shared four sets; grammar only)
- Same Q3 format every year: any 10 of 12 tasks, 1 mark each. Each task sits in a **real-life frame**: a message to a
  friend, a newspaper clipping or report, a line from a school diary's rules, a grandparent's memoir, a company's letter
  to an employee, a poster, a counsellor–student conversation. Our questions copy that idea (frames), never the sentences.
- Frequent targets: future perfect continuous ("This July, you ___ in Japan for three years"), present perfect with
  "since morning", past perfect after "before … could", "Each one of … has", "Neither … nor" agreement, determiners
  (each / all / these), preference ("prefer … to"), modals for past ability / habit (could / would), question tags
  after a past verb, reporting yes/no and wh- questions, statements and thanks.

## Topics (in the order the competency paper groups them)
1. **Tenses**: present / past / future perfect and perfect continuous ("By the end of this quarter she will have been
   working…"), sequence of past events (which happened first), tense in reported narration, conditionals (type 2 and 3).
2. **Modals**: ability, permission, obligation, deduction (must be / can't be), advice (should / ought to), past habit
   (would), regret / criticism (should have, could have), certainty vs possibility (will vs might).
3. **Subject–verb agreement**: as well as / along with / together with vs "and"; each / every / neither…nor / either…or;
   "a number of" vs "the number of"; collective and uncountable nouns (news, jury, cattle); clauses between subject and verb.
4. **Determiners**: all / every / each / both / several / many / few / less vs fewer / this-that-these.
5. **Reported speech**: statements, yes-no and wh- questions (word order), commands and requests (told / asked + to),
   dialogue frames (replied / enquired / remarked / exclaimed), reporting verb in present ("he repeats" → no backshift).
6. **Active and passive**: all tenses incl. present continuous passive, modal passive (must be disinfected), questions in
   passive, when the doer is left out, which part can start the passive sentence.
7. **Clauses and phrases**: relative pronouns (when "that / which" can be dropped), adverbial / noun phrases.
8. **Prepositions**: fixed pairs (prefer … to, superior to, within a time), since vs for, unnecessary prepositions
   ("where is he at", "fell off of").
9. **Editing / error correction** across all of the above: one error per line, or count the errors in a short passage.

## How we turn this into the app (multiple choice, auto-marked)
- "Error + correction" becomes options like "forget → forgotten" (one right pair); "how many errors" stays as 1–4.
- Fill-in tasks become 4 options; reported speech gives full sentences as options.
- Letter writing is not auto-marked: later, model letters as Learn cards (format, tone, structure), no scoring.
- Each topic: Learn cards (rules + examples) → warm-up → practice leaves (easy / board level) → Challenge;
  then "Board practice: Grammar" papers of 12 tasks (10 marks).
