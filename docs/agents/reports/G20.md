## 3. The 5 Least-Sure Questions (and Verification Details)

1. **`sq-matter-sep-q6` (Sublimation of Iodine):**
   - *Question:* "Which solid substance sublimes directly into purple vapour when gently heated?" (Iodine crystals).
   - *Why unsure:* At standard atmospheric pressure, liquid iodine has a narrow range (melts at 113.7°C, boils at 184.3°C), but it readily sublimes at gentle heat into dense purple vapour without visible liquid phase.
   - *Resolution:* Checked standard NCERT/ICSE Class 5 & 6 textbooks and NSO past papers: iodine, camphor, dry ice, and naphthalene are universally cited as the four key examples of sublimation.

2. **`sq-forces-gate-q13` (Crowbar vs Nutcracker Lever Classes):**
   - *Question:* Class 1 (crowbar, fulcrum in middle) vs Class 2 (nutcracker, load in middle).
   - *Why unsure:* Nutcrackers are sometimes confused with tweezers or tongs.
   - *Resolution:* In a nutcracker, the hinge is at the end (fulcrum), the hand squeezes the handles at the opposite end (effort), and the nut rests in between (load). Thus it is strictly a Class 2 lever (Load in middle). Tweezers/tongs have effort in the middle (Class 3).

3. **`sq-earth-gate-q1` (Obsidian Volcanic Glass):**
   - *Question:* Dark volcanic glass rock with razor-sharp edges used for spearheads (Obsidian).
   - *Why unsure:* Is obsidian intrusive or extrusive?
   - *Resolution:* Obsidian is extrusive igneous rock that cools so rapidly upon contact with air or water that mineral crystals do not have time to grow, producing natural amorphous silica glass with conchoidal fractures. Verified accurate for NSO Class 5.

4. **`sq-earth-gate-q7` (Stratospheric Flights for Jets):**
   - *Question:* Why aeroplanes fly in the lower stratosphere (calm, free of weather clouds).
   - *Why unsure:* Long-haul commercial jets cruise between 30,000 and 42,000 feet, which sits at the tropopause / lower stratosphere.
   - *Resolution:* NSO questions frequently ask "Which atmospheric layer provides ideal conditions for flying jet aircraft because it has no weather turbulence?" Expected answer is always Stratosphere.

5. **`sq-earth-gate-q14` (Weight on Moon vs Mass):**
   - *Question:* 66 kg person weighing 11 kg on the Moon.
   - *Why unsure:* In strict physics, mass is measured in kg and weight in Newtons ($W = mg$).
   - *Resolution:* At Class 5 primary level, everyday scales calibrate in kg; textbooks and NSO papers express weight on the Moon as "1/6th of Earth weight in kg" (e.g. "a 60 kg object weighs 10 kg on the Moon"). Explanations explicitly clarify that physical mass remains 66 kg while weight force reduces six-fold.
