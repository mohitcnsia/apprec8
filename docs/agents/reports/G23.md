## 3. The 5 Least-Sure Questions (and Verification Details)

1. **`eq-u5-errors-warmup-q3` (Neither ... nor Subject-Verb Agreement):**
   - *Sentence:* 'Neither my brother nor his friends' (A) / 'is playing football' (B) / 'in the park.' (C) / 'No error' (D).
   - *Why unsure:* When subjects differ in number and are connected by "or/nor", which noun governs the verb?
   - *Resolution:* In standard grammar (and IEO test rubrics), the verb agrees with the closer subject ("his friends" -> plural "are playing"). Part B is unambiguously the erroneous part.

2. **`eq-u5-errors-gate-q12` (Collocational Preposition: 'congratulate'):**
   - *Sentence:* 'The principal congratulated him' (A) / 'for his outstanding victory' (B) / 'in the chess tournament.' (C) / 'No error' (D).
   - *Why unsure:* In Indian English usage, "congratulated for" is often informally heard.
   - *Resolution:* In formal British standard English tested in IEO, one *congratulates someone ON something* (not *for*). Part B is incorrect.

3. **`eq-u5-dialogue-gate-q11` (Negative Politeness with 'Would you mind...?'):**
   - *Prompt:* Priya: "Would you mind closing the window? It is getting chilly."
   - *Why unsure:* Learners frequently choose "Yes, of course" intending to be polite, unaware that answering "Yes" literally means "Yes, I mind / I object".
   - *Resolution:* The proper polite response agreeing to the request is "Not at all, I will shut it right away." This exact trap appears regularly in IEO Class 5 Section 2 questions.

4. **`eq-u5-idioms-warmup-q8` ('Bite the bullet'):**
   - *Prompt:* "Meaning of 'bite the bullet' in context."
   - *Why unsure:* Could children confuse it with literal eating or aggressive behaviour?
   - *Resolution:* Clear options isolate the figurative meaning: "To face an inevitable difficult situation with bravery and determination" vs literal eating, arguing, or fleeing.

5. **`eq-u5-idioms-gate-q8` ('Burn the candle at both ends'):**
   - *Prompt:* "Meaning of 'burn the candle at both ends'."
   - *Why unsure:* The idiom has multiple related senses: wasting resources vs overworking from morning till night.
   - *Resolution:* The primary educational idiom definition tested in school Olympiads is "Working or studying tirelessly from early morning till late night, exhausting oneself." Distractors (wasting electricity, saving money, lighting lamps) eliminate ambiguity.
