## 3. Structural Recommendation: "Longest Option" Tell (Lesson 4)

Lesson 4 of `LESSONS.md` mandates that the right answer must not be the longest option most of the time (`npm test` fails above 40%).

Our unit-by-unit analysis revealed:
- **Unit b1 (Tenses):** 56% longest option tell (62/111 questions).
- **Unit b4 (Reported Speech):** 52% longest option tell (58/111 questions).
- **Unit b5 (Voice):** 42% longest option tell (47/111 questions).
- **Unit b6 (Editing & Prepositions):** 42% longest option tell (47/111 questions).

### Why this happens
In error-correction and dialogue questions, the correct option typically corrects a multi-word clause (35–70 characters), while distractors are single-word trivial changes (`bank → banks`, `ticket → tickets`, `stadium → stadiums`, `asked → said`, 12–18 characters). A student can identify the correct answer simply by choosing the longest option without knowing the grammatical rule.

### Recommendation for Claude
When fixing the error-correction questions, lengthen the distractors to target multi-word phrases of equal length (e.g. `was delayed → had delayed`, `school bus delayed → school bus delaying`), or narrow the correct option's replacement target down to the essential 2-word verb group.
