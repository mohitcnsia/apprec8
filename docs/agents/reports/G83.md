## 3. "Could Be Argued" Cases & Near-Miss Audit

Every question was audited to ensure exactly one defensible answer and at least one tempting near-miss distractor. Below are nuances noted for Claude's review and Seema's review:

1. **`b1` · `eq-b1-perfect-quiz` #6:**
   *Sentence:* `'I have seen that film last Sunday.'`  
   *Distractor:* `Sunday → Sundays`  
   *Nuance:* A student might argue that changing to `Sundays` could imply a recurring habit, but `last Sunday` unambiguously denotes a single finished past event, which requires the simple past `saw`. The rationale clearly explains this.
2. **`b2` · `eq-b2-ability-board` #0:**
   *Sentence:* `'Only registered athletes ___ participate in the trial events tomorrow.'`  
   *Distractor:* `could`  
   *Nuance:* In casual English, `could` is sometimes used for tentative permission, but in an official school sports notice, `can` is the correct modal of formal eligibility.
3. **`b3` · `eq-b3-agreement-board` #7:**
   *Sentence:* `'The jury were unanimous in its decision to acquit the accused.'`  
   *Distractor:* `were → was`  
   *Nuance:* In British English, collective nouns can take plural verbs when members act individually. However, the presence of singular pronoun `its` (`in its decision`) grammatically fixes the jury as a single collective unit, making `was` mandatory.
4. **`b4` · `eq-b4-dialogues-quiz` #0:**
   *Sentence:* `'Do not shout in the corridor.'`  
   *Distractor:* `to not shout`  
   *Nuance:* In casual spoken English, split infinitives (`to not shout`) occur frequently, but CBSE board examiners strictly mark split infinitives as errors, requiring `not to shout`. The rationale notes this.
5. **`b5` · `eq-b5-agent-quiz` #1:**
   *Sentence:* `'Who wrote this poem?'`  
   *Distractor:* `Who was this poem written by?`  
   *Nuance:* In informal modern English, ending a passive question with a dangling preposition (`written by?`) is common. However, CBSE Class 10 board curriculum prescribes the classical passive question inversion `By whom was this poem written?`.
6. **`b6` · `eq-b6-clauses-quiz` #2:**
   *Sentence:* `'The rare medieval manuscript, ___ was discovered in a monastery...'`  
   *Distractor:* `that`  
   *Nuance:* In colloquial English, `that` is often swapped with `which`. In formal board grammar, non-defining relative clauses set off by commas strictly forbid `that` and require `which`.
