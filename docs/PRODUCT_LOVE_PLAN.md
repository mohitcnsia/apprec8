# Why children, parents and teachers will love Apprec8: the plan (Claude, 2026-11-07)

Owner: "We need kids to get hooked and enjoy learning, not dread it. This is still missing. We need a solid plan for
why teachers, parents and kids should and would love the app." Honest view: the app is now **safe, correct and
useful**, but not yet **fun**. Text-heavy pages, plain topic names and few surprises make it feel like homework.

## One promise per person
- **Child:** "10 happy minutes a day, and I can see myself getting better."
- **Parent:** "My child practises what school teaches, safely, and I know how they're doing without nagging."
- **Teacher:** "I know who needs help and what to reteach, with no marking and no extra work."
Everything we build must serve one of these three lines. If it doesn't, we don't build it.

## Children: from homework to a game they come back to
What hooks 6–16-year-olds (and what's missing today):
1. **A world, not a list.** Each unit is a place with a fun name and a little story: "Noun Town", "Verb Volcano",
   "The Tense Time Machine"; Chamak travels with them. The old chapter names were good: bring that spirit back.
   (Missing: names are plain grammar terms.)
2. **Less reading, more doing.** Learn cards everywhere (G59), pictures on every card, read-aloud, and more
   question types: tap the picture, match pairs, sort into boxes, build the sentence. (Missing: mostly text + 4
   options.)
3. **A reason to come back tomorrow.** A **daily quest**: 3 small tasks (one new topic, fix 2 mistakes, one quick
   game) and a surprise at the end. (Missing: streaks exist, but no daily plan.)
4. **Things to collect.** A sticker or card for every topic finished; Chamak outfits; a map that fills with colour.
   (Missing.)
5. **Feeling clever, never stupid.** Celebrate "fixed!" moments in My mistakes; no shaming red screens; hearts and
   timers stay gentle and optional for weaker children. (Partly there.)
6. **Together, safely.** Class team goals ("our class did 500 questions this week!") instead of only rank lists;
   claps from the teacher. (Claps exist; team goal missing.)
Guard rail (mission first): no tricks that make children anxious (streak panic, endless rewards). Calm, short,
happy sessions.

## Parents: peace of mind with zero effort
1. **Starts in one minute** (learn first, row Z): the child learns at once; the parent says yes by WhatsApp link.
2. **Matches school:** "This week Pratha practised the same topics as her Class 5 English book." (Missing:
   topics are not yet linked to school chapters.)
3. **The Sunday summary + one thing to do at home** (exists): keep it short and kind, in English or हिंदी.
4. **Safe screen time:** no ads, no chat, play timer, app lock, helplines (exists).
5. **Free.** Learning stays free (mission-first pricing).

## Teachers: saves time on day one
1. **"Who needs help and what to reteach" on Home** (row Z + G57): one tap to assign practice for the weakest topic.
2. **Linked to the syllabus:** topics tagged with the CBSE / NCERT chapter, so "assign Chapter 4" is one tap.
   (Missing: the biggest reason a teacher would choose us.)
3. **No marking, no setup:** auto-marked practice; parents join with the class code.
4. **Something to show:** a monthly class report for the principal and parents; certificates for children.
   (Missing.)

## What to do, in order
**Phase 1: pilot-ready (next 2 weeks).** Row Z + learn first (built, in review), Learn cards everywhere (G59),
fun names for English units and topics (Gemini proposes, Seema checks, Pratha votes), pictures on Learn cards
for Classes 1–5, celebrations polish. Seema's class pilot + Pratha.
**Phase 2: the hook (weeks 3–6).** Daily quest, stickers / collection, 3 new question types (tap picture, match,
build the sentence), class team goal.
**Phase 3: teachers' reason to choose us (weeks 6–10).** Syllabus tags (CBSE / NCERT chapters) for English
Classes 1–10 and Science 3–8, monthly class report, certificates.

## How we'll know it works (from the pilot)
- Children: do they come back the next day (day-1 return) and after a week (day-7)? Minutes per session, topics
  per week. Watch 3–5 children play for 10 minutes and note where they get bored.
- Parents: do they open the Sunday summary? Would they tell another parent? (one question in the app)
- Teachers: assignments per week; does Seema use it without help after week 1?

## Ask Pratha and Seema first
Before building Phase 2: 5 minutes with Pratha ("what would make you open it tomorrow?") and with Seema ("what
would make you assign it every week?"). Their answers beat any plan.
